Access to
Everyday Life

How transportation, infrastructure, and policy shape people’s ability to participate in daily activities.

Celine Chen • Renée Lee • Heidi Ngo

Access to Everyday Life explores how transportation systems, infrastructure, and policy decisions impact people’s ability to participate in daily activities. Using data visualization and spatial analysis, our project highlights where barriers exist and where targeted improvements can expand opportunity and equity across communities.

Count of Accessibility Barriers

This visualization highlights neighborhoods with the highest number of accessibility barriers, revealing geographic patterns in infrastructure challenges and uneven access to essential services.

Accessibility Burden by Neighborhood

This dashboard measures cumulative accessibility burden by combining multiple barriers into a single index. Higher burden values indicate neighborhoods where transportation limitations, infrastructure gaps, and service access challenges overlap.

Barrier Types Frequency

This visualization shows how frequently different types of accessibility barriers occur, helping identify which obstacles most commonly restrict movement and access across neighborhoods.

Temporary vs. Permanent Barriers by Area

This visualization compares the prevalence of temporary and permanent accessibility barriers across different areas. Distinguishing between short-term and long-term obstacles helps identify where immediate interventions may be effective versus where sustained infrastructure investment is needed.

Mobility Friction by Neighborhood

This visualization illustrates mobility friction across neighborhoods, capturing how environmental, infrastructural, and systemic factors increase the effort required to move through space. Higher friction values indicate areas where accessibility challenges compound, making everyday travel more difficult.
